Case study | Renewable PPA analytics

Solar and wind PPA settlement,
Germany 2020

I settled two virtual PPAs, a 100 MW solar plant and a 150 MW wind farm, hour by hour against the German day-ahead price from January to September 2020, and compared the result with a budget built only from 2019 data. The contracts are hypothetical. Prices and generation profiles are real.

What Finance needs to know

    What I would do next

    • Budget with price scenarios, not a single price path. A low price case would have covered most of this gap.
    • Track capture rate every month as an early warning; it moved before the cost did.
    • Hold payment on the flagged invoices until the counterparty explains the difference.

    06 | Method

    How it was built

    SettlementEvery hour: (market price minus contract price) x generation. Contract prices are 45 EUR/MWh for solar and 50 EUR/MWh for wind. Plant output is the German national capacity factor scaled to each plant.
    Budget and varianceThe 2020 budget uses only 2019 shapes and captured prices. The gap is split into a volume effect (more or less energy) and a price effect (higher or lower captured price).
    Tools and AI usePython, SQL (DuckDB) and Plotly. I used Claude as a coding assistant and checked every result myself: the variance effects add up to the total gap, and the invoice check catches the two errors planted on purpose.
    Data: Open Power System Data, time series 2020-10-06. Solar capacity for 2020 uses the last 2019 value, so solar output may be slightly overstated. Melissa Aristizabal | GitHub
